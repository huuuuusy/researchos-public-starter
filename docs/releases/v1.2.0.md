# ResearchOS Public Starter V1.2

Status: release candidate `v1.2.0-rc.1`, not yet released. The release date is added on release.

The 1.x series is the first stage of ResearchOS: one researcher, local files, decisions at the terminal. What this release adds is how cards are written and how an assistant reads your cards level by level; the behaviour of `ros` changes only in the wording and hints that novice walkthroughs found unclear, and the rules for judgments and admission are unchanged. In the prototype these practices lived only in the author's own material; in this repository each now has a filled-in example, a blank skeleton and a command that runs.

## Added

- **Reading your own cards level by level.** `python3 scripts/plan_context.py <task> --vault personal_vault --mode quick|standard|evidence`, in the vault's language: quick reads the working-style card for that kind of task (the boundary card first for outward tasks), or else the research identity page; standard adds the identity page, the task's workflow and the direction summary, and lists the cards the summary names for the assistant to fetch one by one with `ros context`; evidence adds the outputs and use-boundary index and the author-roles index, and opens only the originals named in these cards' evidence boundaries, for a stated check. Cards not yet in your vault are named, with the skeleton to start from. Each level also says what is not read by default and when to go up a level.
- **Example cards and skeletons.** `examples/starter-cards/en/` and `zh-CN/` hold twelve fictional cards telling one story: research identity page, boundary card, direction summary, project, paper, outputs and use-boundary index, decision, heuristic, working-style card for writing, person, author roles and meeting. `09_templates/basic/` holds blank skeletons for the same cards, plus review comments, grant application, academic message, mentee feedback and mental model. The older full templates are the same cards with sections that are useful later.
- **Working-style cards that grow from corrections.** One per kind of task under `03_cognition/style/` (writing, review, proposal, figures and talks, mentoring). Each point gives the rule, why, how to apply it and its source; when you correct the assistant, it drafts the correction as one point and adds it only after you agree.
- **Boundary card.** `00_identity/boundaries.md`, in your own words: material never given to an assistant, things not said outside, your institution's, ethics board's or journal's rules on AI tools, and who must approve before something goes out. Outward tasks (external requests, proposals, talks, reviews, exports) read it first.
- **Nine research workflows.** `05_workflows/research_work/`: reading papers, writing papers, peer review, grant proposals, meeting follow-up, mentee feedback, changing topic, talks and figures, and retrospectives that turn one lesson into a heuristic card or a working-style point. Three new routes: `peer_review`, `meeting_follow_up`, `mentee_feedback`.
- **Vault check.** The dry run of `python3 scripts/build_public_starter_data.py --personal-vault` reports broken links, unknown sensitivity labels, empty Agent Summaries, trailing `#` comments in frontmatter, direction summaries older than the cards they list, and originals not found on this computer. It only reads; it changes no file.

## Changed

- The First Run writes paper, project and summary cards as `internal` and profile and collaborator cards as `sensitive`. It used to write every card as `private`, which `ros context` never packs, so none of the user's own cards could be handed over.
- The First Run has one more step: the research identity page and one direction summary. `AGENTS.md` has a new section, "Every Session After the First Run": start with `today`, end with `close`; facts are written directly, judgments are submitted with `ros propose` and confirmed by you at the terminal.
- `ros_demo.py --init` adds `02_decisions` and `03_cognition` to the vault's graph roots, and a Chinese vault's fictional commitments are in Chinese.
- `query_research_agenda.py` reads `personal_vault/` by default when it exists, and prints its summary in Chinese for a Chinese vault.
- Card maturity is explained in one place (the card maturity rubric), and the level examples use the fields cards actually carry.
- The manual says which cards the Dashboard shows in personal-vault mode, and has a section for readers who use only a browser chat: without a terminal the recheck after a source change is not available; reading cards layer by layer still is.

Changes to `ros` output after two researchers who had never used a terminal walked through the First Run:

- In a Chinese vault, `ros context` asks in Chinese before handing over a sensitive card; when a card is left out, each one gets a line saying why and what to do (for a sensitive card, run the same command in your own terminal and confirm; it prints there).
- After a judgment block is removed, the hint no longer says to restore it from Git; it says to restore it from your own backup (`personal_vault/` is not in Git).
- The counts in `today` are in plain words: "to do" counts the session's open items and says where those not under "To decide" are and that `today --all` lists them; "To decide" and "Today" count commitments. Items outside the session and the three kinds of unknowns appear only when non-zero.
- After a judgment is replaced, `close` still lists a directly edited `judgments` field. That is by design: a hand edit needs its own confirmation, and confirming the judgment block does not cover it. The hint now says why and prints `ros decide <id> confirm` with the real id; a candidate already confirmed after the fact is no longer listed as undecided.
- The old recheck option labelled "narrow" now reads "keep this item open until the next version (deferred)"; "narrow" means only narrowing a judgment.

Two Dashboard corrections, made under the approvals recorded in the freeze register: in personal-vault mode the fictional demo's card-creation panel is no longer drawn (it wrote no file there), and the material pipeline plugin's default card sensitivity now matches the First Run.

The README now gives about thirty minutes to an hour for the First Run, in line with the two walkthroughs.

The technical report V12 describes `v1.1.0`; this release does not change it.
